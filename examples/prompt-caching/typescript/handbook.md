# Ares Station Crew Handbook

Ares Station is a research station in Arcadia Planitia on Mars, home to a crew of 12. This handbook covers how the station runs day to day. When it and an order from the commander disagree, follow the commander.

## The station

- **Hab A** has the bunks (1 to 12), the lockers, and the laundry.
- **Hab B** has the common room and doubles as the storm shelter. Its walls are lined with water tanks, which block radiation.
- **Galley**: the kitchen and dining room, next to Hab B.
- **Greenhouse**: food crops and the station's only plants.
- **Labs**: the geology lab, the biology lab, and the materials lab, along one corridor.
- **Workshop**: tools, 3D printers, and suit repair.
- **Medbay**: two beds, a pharmacy, and the doctor's office.
- **Airlocks**: Airlock 1 by the workshop for EVAs, Airlock 2 by the garage for the rovers.
- **Power yard**: the solar fields, the battery bank, and the reactor, 400 meters east of the station.

## People

- Commander Lena Hart runs the station and signs off on every EVA beyond 5 km.
- Mateo Ruiz, operations chief, keeps the schedule, the EVA roster, and the rover log.
- Dr. Ada Okafor is the station doctor and reads everyone's radiation dose.
- Jun Park, botanist, runs the greenhouse.
- Sam Novak, engineer, looks after power, life support, and the suits.

## A sol at Ares

A sol, the Martian day, is 24 hours and 39 minutes long. Station clocks run 24 hours, then pause for the 39-minute "timeslip" before midnight.

| Time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 07:00 | Wake up |
| 07:30 | Morning briefing in the common room, 15 minutes |
| 08:00 to 12:00 | First work block |
| 12:00 | Lunch |
| 13:00 to 17:00 | Second work block |
| 17:00 to 18:30 | Exercise. Two hours a sol are required, so most crew add 30 minutes after dinner |
| 18:30 | Dinner |
| 23:00 | Lights out |
| 23:59 | Timeslip, 39 minutes of free time before the clocks restart |

Quiet hours run from 22:00 to 07:00. Music in the common room stops at 21:00.

## Talking to Earth

Signals take between 4 and 24 minutes to reach Earth, depending on where the planets are, so there are no live calls. Record a video message instead.

- The dish talks to Earth in two windows a sol: 09:00 to 10:30 and 20:00 to 21:30.
- Each crew member can send 20 MB of personal messages a sol. Messages from home arrive in the same windows.
- Work traffic always goes first, so personal messages can wait for the next window.
- Every 26 months, Mars passes behind the Sun and the station loses contact with Earth for about two weeks. During this solar conjunction there are no messages at all, and the commander has the final say on everything.

## Life support

The station's air is kept at 70 kPa, a little lower than sea level on Earth, with more oxygen to make up for it. Oxygen comes from splitting water, and scrubbers remove carbon dioxide.

- **Amber alarm**: something is drifting out of range. Finish what you're doing, then check the nearest panel and tell Sam Novak.
- **Red alarm**: air or pressure is unsafe. Stop work, close the hatch behind you, and go to Hab B. If you can't reach Hab B, put on the emergency mask from the ceiling panel of the module you're in and wait.

## Water

The station recycles 98 percent of its water, including what's in the air.

- Each crew member gets 12 liters a sol for drinking, cooking, and washing. Your wristband shows what you've used.
- Showers are 2 minutes long, on Tuesdays and Fridays, booked on the galley board.
- Unused water doesn't carry over to the next sol.

## Greenhouse

The greenhouse grows lettuce, kale, tomatoes, potatoes, herbs, and strawberries under LED lights that run 16 hours a sol. Every crop is weighed and logged, so don't pick anything yourself. Ask Jun Park, who sets aside a snack basket each Sunday. The strawberries are part of an experiment and are never eaten. Each crew member takes a pollination shift once a week, 20 minutes with a paintbrush.

## EVAs

An EVA, extravehicular activity, is any time you go outside.

- Never go out alone. Your buddy is assigned by Mateo Ruiz on the EVA roster.
- An EVA lasts at most four hours, airlock to airlock.
- Before an EVA: book it with Mateo Ruiz a sol ahead, check your suit for 45 minutes with your buddy, then breathe pure oxygen for 30 minutes in Airlock 1.
- Suits come in three sizes and are numbered 1 to 8. Use only your own suit. Report any tear or leak to Sam Novak, and don't patch it yourself.
- Radio check every 15 minutes. If your buddy misses two checks, head back to the airlock.
- No EVAs when the dust in the air is too thick to see the power yard from Airlock 1. If dust rolls in while you're out, stop work and head back together along the marker poles.
- Beyond 5 km you have to take a rover, and the commander has to sign off.

## Rovers

The station has two pressurized rovers, Dune and Mesa. Each seats four and has a range of 60 km, so trips are limited to 30 km out.

- Book rovers in the rover log with Mateo Ruiz.
- Beyond 5 km, the second rover goes too, or stays charged and ready at the station with a driver on call.
- Plug the rover in at the garage charger when you get back, even after a short trip.
- Wear your suit inside the rover whenever you're more than 5 km from the station.

## Radiation

Mars has no magnetic field and only a thin atmosphere, so radiation reaches the ground.

- Wear your dosimeter badge at all times. Dr. Okafor reads it every Monday.
- The limit is 50 millisieverts a month. Crew near the limit are moved to indoor work.
- During a solar particle event, an alert sounds three short beeps. Go to Hab B within 10 minutes and stay until the all-clear, which can take up to a sol.

## Medical

The medbay is open from 08:00 to 17:00, and Dr. Okafor is on call at night on channel 3. Tell her about every injury, even small ones, because cuts heal slowly in low gravity. The pharmacy keeps epinephrine injectors for severe allergies.

## Emergencies

- **Fire**: don't use water. Use the CO2 extinguisher by the hatch, then leave and close the hatch behind you, which starves the fire of oxygen. Call it in on channel 1.
- **Depressurization**: your ears pop and you hear a hiss. The hatches close on their own after 30 seconds, so get out of the module before they do, toward the nearest closed hatch. If you're trapped, put on the ceiling mask and wait for rescue.
- **Muster point**: Hab B for every emergency. The commander takes a headcount.

## Power

Power comes from solar panels and a small fission reactor. In a long dust storm the panels make almost nothing, and the station runs on the reactor alone.

- In power-saving mode, the materials lab and the workshop's 3D printers shut down, the galley cooks one hot meal a sol, and showers are canceled.
- The geology lab, the biology lab, the greenhouse lights, and life support stay on.

## Living together

- Bunks are assigned on arrival. Keep your bunk and locker tidy, since others pass through Hab A.
- Personal items are limited to 1.5 kg, brought on the ship you arrived on.
- Laundry is on Thursdays. Clothes are washed in a shared load, so label everything.
- Recycling: blue bins for plastic, which the workshop turns into 3D printer feedstock; green bins for food scraps, which go to the greenhouse compost; gray bins for everything else.
- Free time at the timeslip is yours. The common room has a film library, board games, and the station's one guitar.
